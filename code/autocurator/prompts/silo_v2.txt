Liquidation. A Silo v2 market is a pair of silos, one per token, created permissionlessly; its configuration (tokens, oracles, rate models, LTVs, fees, hook receiver) is immutable. The vault lends a token through a borrowable deposit in that token's silo; borrowers post the other token in the paired silo. Protected deposits are not lent and earn no interest. The collateral has a max LTV for new borrowing, checked with its max-LTV oracle, and a liquidation threshold lt, checked with its solvency oracle; the market facts show lt as the LLTV. Once a position's LTV exceeds lt, a liquidator can repay debt and receive collateral through the hook receiver. Bad debt is written off only by a hook with a defaulting liquidation, which cancels the debt and deducts the loss from the silo's deposits, so every lender's assets per share falls. Otherwise uncovered debt stays on the books, keeps accruing interest, and utilization can exceed 100 percent.

Oracle. Both tokens are valued in the market's quote token; the market facts give type, subtype and blind_to of the collateral's solvency oracle. The type says whether the oracle reads a market price of the collateral itself; blind_to names the move it cannot see (symbols may be upper-cased). An unseen move leaves the oracle price unchanged and triggers no liquidation. If the collateral's market value falls that way by more than 1 - LLTV, positions at the threshold hold collateral worth less than their debt, borrowers stop repaying, and lenders take the loss when the collateral is redeemed or the debt is written off.
- tracks_market: a fall of the collateral's market price is seen. direct: a market feed or DEX price of the collateral. pt_discount_then_underlying_market: a Pendle PT's TWAP discount against its underlying, then the underlying's market feed.
- exchange_rate: the collateral is priced through a share or issuer rate, never its own market price. A cut in the rate is seen; a market discount against the rate is not. The subtype says what prices the asset the rate converts into. underlying_at_own_feed: its own market feed. underlying_at_other_feed: another asset's feed, blind to a depeg against that asset. underlying_at_par: par, blind to a depeg of the underlying. issuer_reported_price: a rate the issuer reports; no market price is read. underlying_at_nested_oracle: another Silo oracle's quote of the vault asset. pt_underlying_at_par: a PT's Pendle rate, then its underlying at par. pt_then_<subtype>: a PT's Pendle rate, then its underlying priced as that subtype says.
- fixed_price: no collateral move is seen. decimal_scaler: the oracle only rescales decimals, so one collateral unit counts as one quote unit. constant_contract: the oracle returns a constant. no_collateral_input: one collateral unit counts as one unit of the quote numeraire.
- min_of_two (no subtype): the lower of two sources; a fall in either shows once it goes below the other.
- other: proxy_feed_other_asset, a feed of a different asset with no conversion, blind to a depeg against that asset; stored_price, a value an owner or keeper writes, moving only when updated.
- none, no_oracle: the collateral is the quote token and counts one to one; the loan token's own oracle, which this label does not describe, sets the price.
- unresolved: a component could not be identified (the subtype says why); what the oracle sees is unknown.

Rate model. Each silo's rate model is fixed in its configuration. Debt nobody repays keeps accruing interest. Accrued interest is added to the debt and, less the DAO and deployer fees, to the borrowable deposits. With utilization u = debt / borrowable deposits, supply APY = (exp(borrow APR) - 1) x u x (1 - DAO fee - deployer fee). Added supply lowers u and the rate each unit earns.

Withdrawal limits. A lender can withdraw at most the silo's liquidity, borrowable deposits minus debt; a larger withdrawal is filled only up to it. Near 100 percent utilization nothing is liquid until repayment, liquidation or new deposits. A silo whose debt is neither liquidated nor written off stays pinned, and its book value and supply APY overstate what lenders can recover.
