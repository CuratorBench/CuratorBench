Liquidation. Moolah is Lista's fork of Morpho Blue on BNB Chain and applies the same market rules. A market fixes its loan token, collateral token, oracle, interest rate model and LLTV at creation. The oracle gives the price of one collateral unit in loan units. Debt may reach collateral x oracle price x LLTV; above that, a liquidator can repay debt and receive collateral. A fast fall can outrun liquidations and leave debt uncovered. Debt left with no collateral is written off against total supply, so every lender's assets per share falls in proportion. A market can also name a broker, which can write a position's debt off: total borrow and total supply fall together, no collateral is seized, and lenders bear the amount.

Oracle. Most markets use Lista's ResilientOracle, directly or through a forwarding or broker oracle: it reads each token's main feed, checked against a pivot feed, with a fallback feed. The classification describes the main feed. Each market fact gives type, subtype and blind_to for the collateral side of the price. The type says whether the oracle reads a market price of the collateral itself; blind_to names the move it cannot see (symbols may be upper-cased). An unseen move leaves the oracle price unchanged and triggers no liquidation. If the collateral's market value falls that way by more than 1 - LLTV, positions at the threshold hold collateral worth less than their debt, borrowers stop repaying, and lenders take the loss when the collateral is redeemed or the debt is written off.
- tracks_market, direct: a market feed or DEX price of the collateral, so its fall is seen.
- exchange_rate: the collateral is priced through a share or issuer rate, never its own market price. A cut in the rate is seen; a market discount against the rate is not. The subtype says what prices the asset the rate converts into. underlying_at_own_feed: its own market feed. underlying_at_other_feed: another asset's feed, blind to a depeg against that asset. underlying_at_par: par, blind to a depeg of the underlying. issuer_reported_price: a rate the issuer reports; no market price is read.
- fixed_price: no collateral move is seen. constant_contract: the oracle returns a constant. constant_feed: the collateral's feed returns a constant.
- min_of_two (no subtype): the lower of two sources; a fall in either shows once it goes below the other.
- other: proxy_feed_other_asset, a feed of a different asset with no conversion, blind to a depeg against that asset; clamped_market_feed, a market price held between constant bounds, blind to any move beyond them; stored_price, a value an owner or keeper writes, moving only when updated.
- unresolved: a component could not be identified (the subtype says why); what the oracle sees is unknown.

Rate model. Markets use Lista's version of AdaptiveCurveIRM, with Morpho's curve and constants, or a fixed-rate model, which pays its set rate whatever the utilization. With utilization u = total borrow / total supply and a rate at target r_T, the adaptive borrow rate is r_T / 4 at u = 0, r_T at the 90 percent target and 4 x r_T at 100 percent, linear on each side. r_T drifts up above target and down below, faster the farther u is from target; at 100 percent utilization it doubles in about five days. A market may also carry a rate floor and a rate cap that bound the borrow rate. A market fee (0 to 25 percent of interest) is minted as supply shares. Supply APY = (exp(borrow APR) - 1) x u x (1 - fee). Supply the vault adds lowers u, and with it the rate.

Withdrawal limits. A lender can withdraw at most the market's idle liquidity, total supply minus total borrow; a larger withdrawal is filled only up to it. Near 100 percent utilization nothing is idle until repayment, liquidation or new supply. When the collateral no longer covers the debt, borrowers do not repay at any rate: the market stays pinned, interest accrues on debt that may never be paid, and book value and supply APY overstate what lenders can recover.
